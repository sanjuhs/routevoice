\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

RouteVoice establishes a concrete open 180\,nm research milestone: codebook quantization with a small reserved-test point-estimate pass, a full recognizer checked against frozen K64 software on three clips, separately generated index banks, and a routed projection engine that meets setup and hold across nine corners. The recognizer retains INT8 and FP32 components. Its engine passes the reported geometric, equivalence and antenna checks while electrical-limit violations remain.

The most transferable implementation lessons concern local register copies for congestion and pipelining wide selects for slow-corner timing. The recorded AI-assisted workflow combined parallel implementation with direct inspection of tool evidence; it is an experience report rather than a controlled productivity study.

The next phase requires qualified SRAM and array interfaces, a real-time schedule, complete connectivity, density and electrical closure, full-chip verification, and fabrication. Current power estimates exclude the array and complete recognizer. These boundaries make the milestone reproducible and provide concrete targets for further work without claiming a finished voice chip.
